What could a bump next to the anus mean, and how is it typically evaluated?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A bump next to the anus is most often an external hemorrhoid or a small perianal abscess. Other possible causes include anal skin tags, anal warts, or a pilonidal cyst. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam by a healthcare provider, who may gently inspect the area to determine the cause and recommend treatment.

Because these conditions can look similar but require very different care—hemorrhoids often resolve with home remedies, while abscesses may need drainage—identifying the cause early matters. Explanation

When you see a bump near your anus, it could have several causes. One common cause is an external hemorrhoid, which is a swollen blood vessel that can create a visible lump. Another possibility is a perianal abscess, which is a small pocket of infection that can cause pain and swelling in that area. In some cases, other conditions might be responsible for a bump, so it is important not to ignore it. If you're experiencing symptoms like this, you can start by using a free symptom checker to help identify possible causes. To find out what it is, a doctor will likely start with a physical exam where they gently look and feel the area. They might ask questions about any pain, changes in size, or other symptoms you might have. In some cases, if more details are needed, additional tests like an ultrasound could be done to see what's happening under the skin. It's best to have any new or changing bump checked by a healthcare professional, especially if it hurts or grows in size, so you can get the right treatment if needed.
