Is it possible for sleep paralysis to be life-threatening?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep paralysis is not life-threatening, but the experience can feel frightening and unsettling. Episodes are typically brief and harmless, though they may occasionally signal an underlying sleep disorder such as narcolepsy, insomnia, or disrupted sleep patterns. If sleep paralysis happens often, causes anxiety, or is paired with excessive daytime sleepiness, it's worth speaking with a healthcare provider to rule out related conditions.

Sleep paralysis can feel frightening, but it is not dangerous by itself. It happens when you are waking up or falling asleep and your body can't move even though your mind is awake. Some people might feel scared or see things that aren't there, but these feelings go away after a short time.

Not Life-Threatening: Sleep paralysis, itself, is not life-threatening. It is mainly a temporary condition that can be distressing.
