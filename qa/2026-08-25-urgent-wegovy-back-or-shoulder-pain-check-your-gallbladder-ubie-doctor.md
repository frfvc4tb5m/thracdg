Why Gallstones Cause Back or Shoulder Pain

Gallstones are hardened cholesterol or pigment deposits that form in the gallbladder. When they block the cystic or common bile duct, bile builds up, causing inflammation (cholecystitis).

Typical pain patterns:

Upper right abdomen: A steady, sharp pain under the right rib cage.

Right shoulder or between shoulder blades: Referred pain occurs when irritated nerves in the gallbladder share pathways with those areas.

Mid-back pain: Less common but possible if inflammation irritates deeper nerves.

Common Gallstones Symptoms

Not everyone with gallstones has noticeable symptoms. When symptoms do occur, they often include:

Biliary colic: Intense, cramping pain in the upper right belly, lasting 30 minutes to a few hours.

When to Seek Medical Help

While minor digestive complaints often resolve as your body adapts, gallstones can lead to complications:

Acute cholecystitis: Inflammation that may cause fever, severe pain, nausea or vomiting.

Bile duct obstruction: Jaundice, dark urine, pale stools.

Pancreatitis: Sudden, severe mid-epigastric pain radiating to the back.

If you experience any of these warning signs, call your doctor or head to urgent care. You can also use a free Acute Cholecystitis symptom checker to help you assess your symptoms and determine if you need immediate medical attention.

Simple Home Steps While You Wait

If you suspect gallstones but can't see a doctor right away, you can:

Apply a warm compress to the abdomen (avoid heat if skin is broken or very tender).

Sip clear fluids and stick to a low-fat diet to ease gallbladder stimulation.

Take over-the-counter pain relievers (acetaminophen preferred if you have liver concerns; avoid NSAIDs if you have kidney issues or bleeding risks).

Rest and monitor your symptoms closely.

These measures aren't a substitute for professional care—just ways to make yourself more comfortable.

Diagnosis and Treatment

A healthcare provider will typically:

Review your history: Ask about Wegovy use, weight loss pace, and symptom timing.

Physical exam: Check for tenderness in the right upper abdomen.

Ultrasound is the first choice to look for gallstones.

HIDA scan or CT scan if ultrasound is inconclusive.

Blood work: Evaluate liver enzymes, white blood cells and bilirubin.

Possible treatments:

Watchful waiting: For small, painless stones.

Medication: Ursodeoxycholic acid to dissolve cholesterol stones (not always effective).

Surgery: Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal) is the most common and definitive solution.

Endoscopic procedures: ERCP to remove common bile duct stones in urgent cases.

Preventing Gallstones on Wegovy

While you can't eliminate risk entirely, you can take steps to minimize it:

Lose weight at a moderate pace (1–2 pounds per week) when possible.

Eat a balanced diet with healthy fats (olive oil, nuts, avocados) in moderation.

Stay hydrated and include fiber-rich foods.

Discuss supplements or medications with your doctor—some may help reduce gallstone formation.
