Does stress or anxiety trigger sleepwalking episodes?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Stress and anxiety are common triggers for sleepwalking, disrupting the normal sleep cycle and interfering with the deep, non-REM stages of sleep where sleepwalking typically occurs. When stress levels rise, the brain struggles to maintain stable sleep patterns, increasing the likelihood of partial arousals that lead to sleepwalking episodes. Managing stress through relaxation techniques, consistent sleep schedules, and professional support can help reduce the frequency of these episodes.

Explanation

Sleepwalking, also known as somnambulism, is a behavior disorder that originates during deep sleep and results in walking or performing other complex behaviors while asleep. According to the medical literature, stress and anxiety are considered factors that can trigger these episodes.

How Stress and Anxiety Affect Sleepwalking

Stress and Anxiety as Triggers: Stress and anxiety can disrupt the normal sleep cycle, making sleepwalking episodes more likely. These emotional states can cause disturbances during non-REM (NREM) sleep, which is when sleepwalking typically occurs.
