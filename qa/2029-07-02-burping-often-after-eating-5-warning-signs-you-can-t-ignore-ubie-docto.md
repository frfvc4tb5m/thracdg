Why might someone burp often after eating a meal?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Burping after eating is most often caused by swallowing excess air (aerophagia), which occurs when you eat too quickly, talk while chewing, drink carbonated beverages, or chew gum. Certain foods—like beans, onions, and dairy—can also trigger burping, as can conditions such as acid reflux, indigestion, or H. pylori infection.

While occasional burping is normal, frequent or excessive burping paired with pain, bloating, or nausea may point to an underlying digestive issue worth investigating. Because symptoms can overlap across many conditions, self-diagnosing can be tricky—and delaying care can make things worse. Explanation

Burping, also known as belching, is the body's way of releasing excess air from the stomach. After eating, people often burp because they have swallowed air. This can happen if you eat too quickly, talk while eating, or drink carbonated beverages.

Reasons for Burping After Meals:

Air Swallowing: According to research, people can swallow air without realizing it, especially when eating quickly or talking a lot during meals.

Carbonated Drinks: Consuming carbonated drinks can increase the amount of air in your stomach, leading to more burping.
