Why might the upper right side of my abdomen feel numb?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Numbness in the upper right abdomen is often linked to nerve-related issues in that region. Common causes include nerve compression, peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage often tied to diabetes or vitamin deficiencies), or, less commonly, an underlying neurological condition. Because abdominal numbness can also signal problems with nearby organs, identifying the root cause matters.

Explanation

When you feel numbness on the upper right side of your abdomen, it could mean that the nerves sending messages from that area to your brain are not working normally. One possibility is that a nerve might be compressed or irritated by muscle tension, tight clothing, or even a minor injury.

Sometimes, nerve issues can be part of a condition called peripheral neuropathy, where nerves in different parts of the body don't work well due to various factors, including problems with the digestive system. In other situations, numbness may be linked to changes in the central nervous system, like in a case where a person experienced both numbness and word-finding difficulties because of issues in the brain.
