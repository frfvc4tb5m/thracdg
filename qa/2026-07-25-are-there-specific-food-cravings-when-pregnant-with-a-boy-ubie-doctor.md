Are there specific food cravings when pregnant with a boy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Pregnancy cravings are not a reliable indicator of your baby's sex. Despite the popular myth that craving salty, savory, or protein-rich foods means you're having a boy, there is no strong scientific evidence linking specific food cravings to fetal gender. Research suggests pregnancy cravings are driven by hormonal shifts, changing nutritional needs, individual taste preferences, and personal or cultural experiences—not by whether you're carrying a boy or a girl.

Explanation

Research shows that while many women report unusual food cravings during pregnancy, these cravings do not seem to follow a clear pattern based on whether you are carrying a boy or a girl.

[food cravings when pregnant](https://peplawllc.com/blog/5636491348.html)

Studies have looked at women's experiences and found that cravings are mainly influenced by changes in hormones and individual taste preferences. For example, cravings for sweets, salty snacks, or even unusual food combinations are common and can be very personal.

Some studies suggest that the way a woman experiences food cravings is more linked to her overall mood, culture, and the body's nutritional needs rather than the sex of the baby. Focus group studies have noted that many parents experience shifts in eating behaviors during the transition to parenthood, stemming from many different factors and not necessarily the baby's gender.
