What should patients expect during a typical urgent care visit?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Urgent care visits typically involve a fast, focused evaluation for non-life-threatening conditions such as minor injuries, infections, fevers, and flu-like symptoms. Patients can expect shorter wait times than the emergency room, on-site diagnostics like X-rays or lab tests, and same-day treatment or prescriptions. Most visits last 30–60 minutes, and no appointment is usually needed.

Explanation

When visiting an urgent care center, patients should expect a streamlined experience focused on addressing non-life-threatening medical issues. These centers are designed to provide quick and efficient care for conditions that need attention sooner than a regular doctor's appointment but are not emergencies.

What to Expect:

Check-In: Patients will first check in at the front desk, providing information about their symptoms and medical history. Before heading to urgent care, you can use Ubie's free AI-powered symptom checker to better understand your symptoms and determine if urgent care is the right level of care for your situation.

Waiting Time: There might be a short wait, but urgent care centers aim to minimize this to provide timely care.

Consultation: A healthcare provider will see the patient to assess their condition. This includes a brief examination and questions about symptoms.

Treatment: Depending on the issue, treatment may be given on the spot, or patients might receive prescriptions or advice for home care.

Follow-Up: Instructions for follow-up care or further testing, if needed, will be provided.

Urgent care centers prioritize efficiency and accessibility, making them a convenient option for many patients. They offer an alternative to emergency rooms for less severe health issues, helping patients receive care without long waits or the need for an appointment.
