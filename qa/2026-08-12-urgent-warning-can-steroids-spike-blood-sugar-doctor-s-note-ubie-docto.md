# Urgent Warning: Can Steroids Spike Blood Sugar? | Doctor’s Note | Ubie Doctor's Note

Can steroids increase blood sugar?

[Note Ubie Doctor's Note](https://benpepsi.com/blog/0878068829.html)

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, steroids can raise blood sugar levels, even in people without diabetes. Corticosteroids like prednisone, dexamethasone, and hydrocortisone reduce insulin sensitivity and prompt the liver to release more glucose, leading to elevated blood sugar known as steroid-induced hyperglycemia.

Key facts:

Blood sugar spikes can occur within hours of starting steroids.

Higher doses and longer treatment increase the risk.

Symptoms may include increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, and blurred vision.

Non-diabetics can develop temporary or, in some cases, long-term diabetes.

Explanation

Steroids, also known as corticosteroids, are medications that can help with inflammation and other issues. However, they can also cause blood sugar levels to rise. According to research, even people who do not have diabetes can experience higher blood sugar levels when taking steroids.

How Steroids Affect Blood Sugar:

Steroid Types: Different types of steroids, whether taken by mouth or through an IV, can affect blood sugar levels.

Non-Diabetic Patients: Studies have shown that even people without diabetes can have glucose disturbances when on steroids.

Corticosteroid-Induced Diabetes: Long-term use of steroids may lead to a condition known as corticosteroid-induced diabetes, where blood sugar levels become consistently high.
