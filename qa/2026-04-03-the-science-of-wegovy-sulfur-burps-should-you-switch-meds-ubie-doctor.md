Explanation

Wegovy (semaglutide) has become a popular prescription for long-term weight management. As a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, it helps control appetite and blood sugar. However, some users report sulfur burps—burps that smell like rotten eggs. If you're asking "why does Wegovy cause sulfur burps?" you're not alone. This guide explains the science, offers management tips, and helps you decide whether it's time to consider another medication.

What Are Sulfur Burps?

Sulfur burps are belches with a distinct sulfur or "rotten egg" odor. They happen when hydrogen sulfide gas (H₂S) is released in your stomach or intestines, then exhaled. Common triggers include:

High-protein or sulfur-rich foods (eggs, red meat, cruciferous vegetables)

Gastrointestinal infections (H. pylori, giardia)

Malabsorption or small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)

Certain medications, including GLP-1 agonists

Why Does Wegovy Cause Sulfur Burps?

Delayed Gastric Emptying• Wegovy slows stomach emptying to prolong feelings of fullness.• Slower transit time can allow food to stay in the stomach longer, creating an environment for bacterial fermentation.

Increased Fermentation• As undigested food lingers, gut bacteria break down proteins and sulfur-containing amino acids.• The byproduct, H₂S gas, is released and expelled as sulfur burps.

Changes in Gut Motility• GLP-1 receptor activation can alter gut motility and pH, favoring certain bacteria that produce more H₂S.

Individual Variation• Your baseline gut flora, diet, and digestive health influence how pronounced sulfur burps become.

Understanding this mechanism answers "why does Wegovy cause sulfur burps?"—it's largely due to slowed digestion and increased bacterial activity.

How Common Are Sulfur Burps on Wegovy?

Clinical trials and real-world reports suggest gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, bloating, diarrhea) are common. Sulfur burps specifically are less frequently documented but still occur for a subset of patients:

Up to 20% of GLP-1 users report some form of burping or belching.

Fewer than 5% mention a distinct sulfur smell.

If you experience mild burping, it may improve over weeks. Persistent or severe sulfur burps warrant attention.

Managing Sulfur Burps Without Switching

Before considering a medication change, try these strategies:

Dietary Adjustments

Reduce sulfur-rich foods: eggs, garlic, onions, broccoli, cauliflower.

Favor easily digestible proteins: fish, poultry, tofu.

Eat smaller, more frequent meals to ease gastric workload.

Meal Timing and Portion Control

Take Wegovy at the same time each day, ideally with a light meal.

Avoid large meals or heavy proteins within 2–3 hours after your injection.

Over-the-Counter Remedies

Bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) can neutralize gas and odor.

Activated charcoal tablets may trap gases in the digestive tract.

Simethicone (Gas-X) can reduce bloating and belching.

Probiotics and Digestive Enzymes

Probiotics with Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus may rebalance gut flora.

Digestive enzyme supplements (protease, lipase) can aid protein breakdown.

When to Think About Switching Medications

If sulfur burps persist or significantly impact your quality of life, it may be time to discuss alternatives. Consider switching if:

Burps remain severe despite dietary and OTC interventions.

You experience weight loss plateaus due to reduced food intake (fear of burping).

You develop additional intolerable side effects (vomiting, dehydration).
