What is Gastritis?

Gastritis is defined as a set of symptoms caused by inflammation in the lining of the stomach. These symptoms can range from mild to severe, and gastritis can either be acute, lasting a few hours to a few days, or chronic, ongoing for months at a time.

Because of the number of causes and symptoms, there is no specific treatment for gastritis, but instead, a series of things that one can do to minimize symptoms and improve outcomes from episodes.

What Causes Gastritis

Gastritis has several causes; certain ones are more common than others.

Bacterial infections that irritate and erode the stomach lining are the most common cause, as different harmful bacteria can cause inflammation, nausea, and vomiting.

Excessive consumption of alcohol is also known to cause inflammation of the stomach lining. This irritation is the reason for many of the symptoms people experience when they have a hangover.

Certain pain medications, both prescriptions and over-the-counter medications, can cause irregularities with stomach acid and erosion of the stomach lining, which can lead to chronic and severe gastritis.

Certain immune disorders can cause gastritis, and these may be difficult to treat without addressing the underlying condition. Patients may struggle with symptoms for long periods of time or even go undiagnosed if they do not seek treatment from a doctor.

Gastritis Symptoms

Gastritis can cover a wide range of symptoms or can be symptomless. Most people report problems such as stomach discomfort, nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea.

In rare cases, gastritis can cause bleeding in the stomach, severe pain, and/or blood in the stool or vomit.

The exact symptoms a person has will depend on their type of gastritis, the cause of their condition, and underlying health and environmental factors. People can have mild gastritis and then, thanks to things like stress, can develop stomach ulcers and bleeding in the stomach, which can compound with the issues caused by gastritis.

This makes it important to always monitor your symptoms; if they get worse without an explanation, see a doctor immediately.
