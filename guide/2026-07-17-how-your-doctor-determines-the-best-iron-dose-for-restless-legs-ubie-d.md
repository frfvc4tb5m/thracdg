How Your Doctor Determines the Best Iron Dose for Restless Legs

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Doctors diagnose iron deficiency in restless legs syndrome (RLS) by testing serum ferritin, transferrin saturation, hemoglobin, and inflammation markers. The goal is a ferritin level of at least 50 ng/mL to support dopamine production and ease symptoms.

Treatment typically starts with 65–100 mg of elemental iron nightly (such as ferrous sulfate), with labs and symptom diaries reviewed at 8–12 weeks. If absorption is poor or symptoms persist, doctors may adjust the dose or switch to IV iron.

Because restless legs symptoms can overlap with other conditions—and iron levels aren't the only factor—it's worth understanding your full symptom picture before your next appointment. 2. Initial Evaluation: Blood Tests

Before prescribing any iron, your doctor will check:

Serum ferritin• Optimal target for RLS is usually ≥ 50 ng/mL (some guidelines aim for 75–100 ng/mL).

Transferrin saturation (TSAT)• Reflects how much iron is bound and ready for use; values < 20% suggest deficiency.

Hemoglobin (Hb) and hematocrit (Hct)• Rule out anemia (Hb men < 13 g/dL, women < 12 g/dL).

C-reactive protein (CRP) or other markers of inflammation• Inflammation can raise ferritin independently of iron stores.

If tests show low ferritin or TSAT—even without full anemia—your doctor may recommend iron supplements.

4. Typical Iron Supplements Dosage for RLS

General dosing guidelines for adults with RLS and low ferritin:

Start with 65–100 mg elemental iron once daily, usually in the evening

If tolerated, some doctors split the dose to twice daily (e.g., 50 mg morning, 50 mg evening)

Newer research suggests single nightly dosing may boost absorption and reduce side effects

Key points:

Take on an empty stomach (1–2 hours before or after meals) for best absorption.

If GI upset occurs, take with a small amount of food—but avoid dairy, tea, coffee, or calcium supplements around dosing.

Adding vitamin C (250 mg) can help increase iron uptake.

5. Monitoring Progress

After starting iron therapy:

Re-check ferritin and TSAT in 8–12 weeks.

Assess symptom improvement using your own diary or sleep log.

Adjust dose if ferritin is still below target or if side effects limit adherence.

Once ferritin hits the goal (often ≥ 50 ng/mL):

Maintain a lower "maintenance dose" (e.g., 30–60 mg elemental iron daily) for several more months.

Periodically re-check iron labs every 6–12 months.

6. When Oral Iron Isn't Enough

Some patients need intravenous (IV) iron, such as:

Persistent ferritin < 50 ng/mL despite adequate oral dosing

Medication or gastrointestinal conditions that impair absorption (e.g., inflammatory bowel disease, gastric bypass)

Severe RLS symptoms causing significant sleep loss or disability

Chronic kidney disease on dialysis

IV iron formulations (e.g., ferric carboxymaltose, iron sucrose) deliver a large iron load quickly. Your doctor will choose the product, total dose, and infusion schedule based on safety data and your medical history.

8. Lifestyle and Dietary Tips

While iron supplements work, you can also:

Eat more lean red meat, poultry, fish

Include legumes, fortified cereals, spinach (with vitamin C source)

Avoid iron blockers around meals: coffee, tea, dairy, calcium supplements

Maintaining a balanced diet supports long-term iron levels and overall health.

Iron dosing and RLS management must be tailored individually. Your doctor will integrate lab results, symptom severity and overall health to find the safest, most effective strategy.

Restless Legs Syndrome can improve greatly when underlying iron deficiency is identified and treated correctly.
