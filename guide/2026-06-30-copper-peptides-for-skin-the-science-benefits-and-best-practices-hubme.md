# Copper Peptides for Skin: The Science, Benefits, and Best Practices | Hubmed

Copper peptides have emerged as a powerful ingredient in modern skincare, offering a science-backed approach to skin regeneration and anti-ageing. Known for their many abilities to improve skin health, these bioactive compounds are widely used in both professional treatments and everyday skincare products.

Their effectiveness extends beyond basic hydration and nourishment, actively repairing damaged skin cells and enhancing overall skin health. As a key component in peptide therapy, copper peptides support making them a valuable tool in aesthetic medicine.

Overview of Copper Peptides

Copper peptides are naturally occurring molecules that contribute to skin regeneration and overall cellular function. In 1973, Loren Pickart (1938–2023) discovered and isolated the copper peptide GHK-Cu from human plasma albumin. Since then, they have been extensively studied for their ability to heal wounds, reduce inflammation, and stimulate collagen synthesis.

In professional skincare, copper peptides are incorporated into various treatments, from microneedling serums to post-procedure recovery products. Aestheticians and skin care professionals interested in learning more about these advanced treatments can benefit from a peptide training course. The Science Behind Copper Peptides

Copper peptides are small protein fragments that naturally bind to copper ions, forming bioactive complexes that play a crucial role in skin health, including:

Cellular interaction and skin regeneration: The primary function of copper peptides in skincare revolves around their ability to signal fibroblasts - cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. Collagen provides structural support to the skin, while elastin maintains its flexibility and resilience.

Additionally, copper peptides influence keratinocytes - the cells responsible for skin renewal.

Wound healing: Another critical function of copper peptides is their role in tissue repair and wound healing. They stimulate the production of glycosaminoglycans, such as hyaluronic acid, which helps retain moisture and supports tissue hydration. Copper peptides stimulate angiogenesis, the process of forming new blood vessels, which improves oxygen and nutrient supply to damaged skin, accelerating the healing process.

Anti-inflammatory properties: Their anti-inflammatory properties further contribute to skin health by reducing redness, irritation, and swelling. This makes copper peptides particularly beneficial for individuals dealing with conditions such as acne scars, sun damage, and post-procedure recovery.

Antioxidant protection: GHK copper peptide, in particular, functions as an antioxidants, neutralising free radicals that cause oxidative stress and premature ageing. By reducing oxidative stress, copper peptides help protect the skin from external aggressors, preserving its youthful appearance.

Skin barrier strengthening: these peptides play a role in strengthening the skin’s protective barrier. A healthy skin barrier prevents moisture loss, shields against irritants, and enhances the skin’s ability to retain essential nutrients. This makes copper peptides a valuable ingredient for individuals with dry, sensitive, or compromised skin.

The role of copper ions in skin function: The presence of copper itself is essential for various enzymatic functions in the skin. Copper-dependent enzymes, such as lysyl oxidase, contribute to the stabilisation of collagen and elastin, ensuring the skin remains firm and supple. Copper is also involved in melanin production, which regulates pigmentation and helps repair UV-induced skin damage.

Copper peptides and hair growth: Beyond skincare, copper peptides have been studied for their role in promoting hair growth. They extend the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle, strengthen hair follicles, and improve scalp circulation. The best copper peptides for hair growth, such as copper peptide -1, stimulate follicular activity, making them a promising treatment for thinning hair and hair loss.

What Are the Downsides of Copper Peptides?

While generally well-tolerated, there are some potential downsides to using copper peptides:

Skin sensitivity: Some users experience redness or irritation, especially when first incorporating copper peptides into their routine.

Overuse can disrupt skin balance: Excessive application may lead to breakouts or an imbalance in natural skin functions.

Potential for adverse reactions: Rare cases of users reporting issues often stem from misuse or incompatibility with other ingredients.

The recommendation is to start with a lower concentration and gradually increasing usage can help minimize these risks.

Precautions for Having Copper Peptide Treatments

Before undergoing professional copper peptide treatments, consider the following precautions:

Patch test before use: Apply a small amount of the product to your inner forearm and wait 24 hours to check for any redness, itching, or irritation, especially if you have sensitive skin.

Avoid overuse: Using high concentrations too frequently can overstimulate collagen production, potentially leading to skin imbalance, breakouts, or sensitivity. Follow product recommendations and introduce copper peptides gradually.

Do not use with exfoliating acids: Strong acids like glycolic acid, lactic acid, and salicylic acid can break down peptides and reduce their effectiveness. If using AHAs or BHAs, apply them at different times of the day.
