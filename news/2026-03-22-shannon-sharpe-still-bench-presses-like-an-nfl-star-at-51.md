# Shannon Sharpe Still Bench Presses Like an NFL Star at 51

Shannon Sharpe turned 51 earlier this year, and is still in as good shape as he's ever been.

[Bench Presses](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/1357925655.html)

The NFL Hall of Famer looks like he could even suit up right now, pressing easy bench reps at 225 pounds.

Instead, he saves his energy for debating Skip Bayless on Fox Sports One.

Shannon Sharpe hasn't played professional in nearly 20 years, but even at 51, he's showing that he can still train with the best the National Football League has to offer.

The NFL Hall of Famer and three-time Super Bowl winner (he won a pair with John Elway's Denver Broncos, and one as a part of the 2000 Baltimore Ravens) is best known these days for his role as the voice of reason when debating commentator Skip Bayless' often outlandish sports takes on Fox Sports One's Skip and Shannon: Undisputed, but from the looks of his workouts, it looks like he could easily turn the clock back two decades and suit up for the gridiron if he so wanted to.

"Today was a rep day," he wrote on his Instagram, before sharing his exact workout and posting a video of his bench press routine. "225x20, 275x15, 315x10 30 degree incline 90x12, 105x12, 120x12 3 sets of seated shoulder press 80x10 90x10 100x10, Side lateral raises, front lateral raise 3x12 for both. Now resting for 4 pm CrossFit," he wrote.

Last week, he also posted his bench routine. "Today was heavy bench day," he wrote, before listing his splits again. Even more impressively, these bench press sets were just workout number one for the day, as Sharpe was waiting on his afternoon CrossFit session.

Sharpe doesn't post his workouts to Instagram too often, sticking mostly to videos from his show, memes, and posts that show off his pieces from impressive bow tie collection. But when he does, he shares his routine, and always gathers the envy of some of his fans and peers. Mr. Olympia winner and professional strongman Dexter Jackson and rapper Busta Rhymes both commented bicep emojis on Sharpe's heavy bench workout.

Last year, Sharpe spoke to Men's Health about his workout routine and staying in shape even when (at the time) he was approaching the big 5-0. He made clear that every time he steps into the gym, he leaves nothing behind. “I tell people, when I train I don’t do a Hansel and Gretel workout,” he said. “I don’t drop breadcrumbs. I saved nothing for the trip home.”

He also made clear that he doesn't switch things up too much—he's found a routine he likes, and he doesn't mess with it. He knows what works for him. “They say that insanity is doing something over and over again, but [expecting] different results,” Sharpe said. “I do the same thing over and over for the same result—to be healthy and happy at the end of the day.”

If you're going to try to keep in shape as consistently and efficiently as Sharpe does, you'll need to make sure you're bench pressing the right way. As Men's Health Fitness Director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. notes, that means three things: keeping your feet flat on the floor, keeping your shoulders in the correct position (with weight directly above), and doing your best to keep elbows at a 45 degree angle, lower than your shoulders.
