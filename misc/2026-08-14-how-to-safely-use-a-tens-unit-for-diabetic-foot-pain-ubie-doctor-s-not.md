What Is a TENS Unit?

A TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) unit is a small, battery-powered device that sends mild electrical pulses through surface electrodes placed on the skin. These pulses:

Stimulate nerve fibers

Distract the brain from pain signals

Encourage the release of natural endorphins

Unlike medical procedures or medications, TENS is non-invasive and can be used at home under proper guidance.

How a TENS Unit Helps Neuropathy

When you have diabetic neuropathy, damaged nerves in your feet send abnormal pain signals to the brain. Using a TENS unit for neuropathy may:

Block pain transmission: Electrical pulses interrupt pain signals traveling to the spinal cord and brain.

Boost endorphins: The body's natural painkillers can reduce discomfort.

Improve circulation: Mild electrical stimulation may encourage blood flow to the feet.

Choosing the Right TENS Unit

Not all TENS devices are the same. Look for features geared toward neuropathy relief:

Adjustable intensity levels (low to moderate)

Multiple modes (e.g., burst, continuous, modulation)

Timer settings (10–60 minutes)

Quality, reusable electrodes

Clear user manual and safety instructions

Consult product reviews and, if possible, ask your healthcare provider for recommendations. Avoid very cheap, unbranded units without clear specifications.

Safety First: Precautions and Contraindications

Using a TENS unit is generally safe, but it's important to take precautions:

Do not place electrodes over broken or irritated skin.

Keep pads at least 1 inch apart; do not overlap.

Over the chest (risk of affecting heart rhythm)

Over the carotid arteries (neck)

On the head or eyes

A pacemaker or other implanted electronic device

Epilepsy (unless approved by a neurologist)

Open wounds or severe skin conditions

Always start at the lowest intensity and increase slowly.

Stop immediately if you feel burning, numbness or increased pain.

Step-by-Step Guide to Using a TENS Unit for Diabetic Foot Pain

Read the manualFamiliarize yourself with your model's features, controls and safety warnings.

Clean your skinWash and dry your feet. Remove lotions or oils so electrodes adhere properly.

Position the electrodes

For plantar (sole) pain: place one pad near the arch and one near the heel.

For top-of-foot pain: place pads on either side of the painful area.

Maintain at least 1 inch of space between pads.

Select a program

Continuous mode: steady pulse rate, good to start.

Burst mode: intermittent pulses, may feel more like tapping.

Modulation mode: varies pulse intensity and frequency to prevent "nerve accommodation."

Set the timerBegin with 15–20 minutes per session. You can increase to 30–45 minutes if well tolerated.

Adjust intensitySlowly raise intensity until you feel a strong but comfortable tingling. It should never be painful.

Relax and monitorSit or lie down in a comfortable position. Check every 5–10 minutes to ensure no burning or skin irritation.

Turn off and removeAfter the timer ends, lower intensity to zero, switch off the unit, then gently peel off electrodes.

Tips for Maximizing Benefits

Use TENS daily or as recommended, but avoid sessions longer than 60 minutes.

Combine TENS with gentle foot stretches and regular blood sugar control.

Track your pain before and after sessions to gauge effectiveness.

Rotate electrode placement slightly each day to prevent skin irritation.

Keep your feet warm; cold can worsen neuropathy symptoms.

Recognizing When to Adjust or Stop

While TENS is safe, pay attention to your body's signals:

Unchanged or worsening pain after several sessions may mean TENS isn't right for you.

Skin redness or blisters under electrodes requires a break and possible pad replacement.

New feelings of dizziness or nausea during use means stop immediately and seek advice.

If you have any serious or life-threatening symptoms—such as chest pain, severe foot ulcers, sudden numbness or signs of infection—stop TENS and speak to a healthcare professional right away.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

Even if your TENS sessions feel safe and beneficial, you should:

Review your TENS plan with your healthcare provider, especially if you have heart or nerve conditions.

Report any worrying side effects, such as persistent skin changes or new pain patterns.

Seek immediate help for any foot wounds, infections or sudden loss of sensation.

Speak to a doctor about anything that could be life threatening or serious. Proper medical guidance ensures you get the best possible care for diabetic neuropathy.
