# You’re Spending Too Much on Longevity. Here’s What Science Says Actually Extends Your Life.

[Here What Science](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/9916251026.html)

This story is part of Fit at Any Age: New Frontiers—a next-level guide to the latest science and tips for a stronger, longer life.

TECH CENTIMILLIONAIRE Bryan Johnson, a self- described “rejuvenation athlete,” reportedly spends more than $2 million a year on everything from $25,000 gene-therapy injections to 111 pills per day. (He’s offering his supplement stack for a $333-per-month subscription.)

[What Science Says Actually](https://allpepay.com/)

It’s kind of working. Johnson, 46, is number seven on the Rejuvenation Olympics scoreboard (the top 20 people, out of about 1,750 contestants, with the greatest difference between chronological and biological ages based on DNA methylation, called an epigenetic clock). But others have achieved similar results on much lower budgets. Most notably, number eight on the scoreboard: Julie Gibson Clark, a 55-year-old recruiter whose routine involves regular exercise (a $27-a-month gym membership), daily meditation, three saunas a week, a vegetable- rich diet, and $79 monthly in supplements. Currently, she’s aging at a rate of 0.65 of a year for every additional chronological year, to Johnson’s 0.69. (Normal aging is 1.) So how much do we truly need to spend on longevity? “There are a lot of really interesting drugs in the pipeline that will unquestionably make a difference in length and quality of life,” says Dr. Attia. But we’re not quite there yet. In the meantime, use this chart to guide your spending.

Exercise

[Your Life](https://pigmentlab.jcesqc8.com/)

“More than anything else, exercise has the greatest power to determine how you will live out the rest of your life,” says Dr. Attia. It can slow or reverse physical and cognitive decline. Investments—trackers, trainers, gear—that help you work out consistently will likely pay off.

Sleep

Quality sleep is essential to an optimal health span. Trackers can push you to prioritize sleep, but they’re not a must, as the data can be confusing and the accuracy is still in question. Instead, assess your sleep with a questionnaire like the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index.

Blood Sugar

Glucose control is also critical to health span. “If you’re one of the many people in a blood-sugar gray zone—you have an average blood glucose between 120 and 140 mg/dL—using a continuous glucose monitor can be a powerful tool to identify and address problems,” he says.

Rapamycin

[Your Life](https://healingkinetics.bbsxyxy.com/blog/4535358192.html)

Intermittent doses of this drug may help your body get rid of its oldest and weakest cells. “It’s the most promising geroprotective agent we have at this time,” Dr. Attia says, meaning one that would help you live longer but not by addressing a specific disease.

Saunas

Based on the data, taking a sauna four times per week for 20 minutes at about 180 degrees Fahrenheit has brain, heart, and mortality- risk benefits. Taking one at night can also lead to much better sleep, which Dr. Attia believes may explain some of those benefits.

SGLT-2 Inhibitors

Drugs called sodium-glucose cotransporter protein-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors—there are four that are FDA approved—help lower blood sugar and treat diabetes. “The data on SGLT-2 inhibitors is getting better and better with each subsequent study, and each generation of those drugs seems to offer more and more geroprotection,” says Dr. Attia. “Along with SGLT-2s’ nameplate stated benefits, we’re seeing these remarkable benefits in terms of kidney failure, heart failure, and things like that. So SGLT-2s are transcending their stated purpose. I think we’ll see more people without diabetes eventually taking SGLT-2 inhibitors, because the data shows a significant survival advantage in terms of outright mortality.”

Metformin

Research on whether this diabetes drug can protect against aging has turned up enough varied evidence to suggest it might—there are just nuances that need to be pinned down. “I think there’s enough smoke there that we should see if there’s fire,” Dr. Attia says.

Aging Clocks

Algorithms use methylation and protein markers on your DNA to calculate biological age. “Last year, I would have put these in Noise,” Dr. Attia says. But now he thinks down the road, clocks may determine how lifestyle changes can slow or accelerate aging.

Ice Baths

There’s an anti-inflammatory benefit and there’s the emotional health boost—you’re doing something difficult that can lead to feelings of satisfaction and improved mood. But will ice baths help you live longer, as many claim? Probably not, says Dr. Attia.

NAD+

This naturally occurring molecule in your body aids cell functioning. Decreased levels with aging are associated with cognitive decline, cancers, and loss of muscle. There’s not enough data from human studies that reveal a longevity benefit.

Young Blood Transfusions

[Much on](https://satietybrief.china-akan.com/blog/7874914435.html)

There’s no evidence that these can prevent aging in older people. It’s an interesting idea that needs a lot more research. Red flag: lots of commercial activity despite zero human data.

Resveratrol

“Probably one of the biggest cons in the supplement industry,” Dr. Attia says. Despite all of the research, “only one experiment has ever suggested that resveratrol did anything.”

Red-Light Therapy

For certain skin conditions, there are benefits, but there’s a lack of evidence indicating any geroprotective upsides.

What Should Be in a Longevity Stack of Supplements?

NO SUPPLEMENT WILL ever replace the longevity-plan fundamentals of exercising regularly, getting enough quality sleep, and eating at least five servings of fruits and vegetables daily. Not even close. However, taking some supplements in order to support those fundamentals can help, according to Brian St. Pierre, M.S., R.D., C.S.C.S., Director of Nutrition at Precision Nutrition. Here's what he thinks is worth it:

MULTIVITAMIN

A multivitamin with around 100 (not 1,000+) percent DV helps prevent deficiency in important nutrients.

VITAMIN D

1,000 to 2,000IU contributes to an adequate blood level for immune health.

PROTEIN POWDER

Meeting your protein needs helps your body burn fat and build/retain muscle.

MAGNESIUM
