# This Split Squat Variation Will Make Your Glutes Explode

The Bulgarian split squat is one of the finest unilateral leg exercises out there, simultaneously challenging strength, power, and balance as you lower your torso on one leg, the other held a few feet above the ground, then stand back up.

But what if you could make it harder, and get even more glute stabilization benefit. And what if it could build back power and glute power too? That's what you get with the Bulgarian Pass-Through to Clean from Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. Samuel's new take on the Bulgarian split squat gives you all the benefits of the standard split squat, and it lets you make your body more explosive, too. All the while, you'll improve your balance and challenge your core more than you expect.

A bonus: You'll do it all without beating your body up with heavy weights. "The key here is gonna be offset loading," says Samuel, "and the changes we'll make to that offset load as we go. And it'll catch your body off-guard, requiring mental focus and a lot more stability than you're used to."

The pass-through clean has you lowering into a Bulgarian split squat, then passing a dumbbell or kettlebell through your legs to the other side. From there, you clean it to your shoulder. This transition, says Samuel, challenges your core and glutes to stabilize your torso. "If you don't do that," he says, "you'll tip to one side or lose your balance."

The clean element, meanwhile, builds explosion. "It's not a biceps curl to your shoulder," says Samuel. "It's a powerful, aggressive clean, driven by back muscle and the slightest bit of hip hinge." Your body will love the changeup, so get ready to rock.

Start with your left leg on a bench or chair, positioned for a Bulgarian split squat. Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell at your right shoulder, core tight.

Bend your front knee, lowering until your thigh is nearly parallel with the ground.

Lower the weight from your shoulder, and pass it through your legs to the left arm.

Lean forward slightly, keeping your core tight, then hinge backwards explosively; use that momentum to drive the weight to your shoulder height.

Stand back up. That's 1 rep. Repeat the process.

Do 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps per side.

It can also be a solid warmup move, cueing your body to be explosive and teaching glute and core control. Or it can be a changeup workout you throw in a total body circuit. The possibilities for the move are endless, so get it in your training routine.
