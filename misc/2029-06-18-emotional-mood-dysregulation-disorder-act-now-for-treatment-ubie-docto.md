What is emotional mood dysregulation disorder and how is it treated?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD), sometimes called emotional mood dysregulation disorder, is a childhood mental health condition marked by chronic, severe irritability and frequent, intense temper outbursts that are out of proportion to the situation. Symptoms typically appear before age 10 and can significantly impact a child's home, school, and social life. Treatment usually combines behavioral therapy, family support, and, in some cases, medication to help regulate mood and reduce outbursts.

If your child shows signs of persistent irritability or explosive anger, understanding the underlying cause is the crucial first step toward getting effective help. Because DMDD shares symptoms with other conditions like ADHD, anxiety, and bipolar disorder, guessing can delay proper care. Explanation

Emotional mood dysregulation disorder, or Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD), affects children and is marked by extreme irritability, anger, and frequent, intense temper outbursts. These outbursts are more severe than expected for the child's age and occur regularly.

Treatment Options:

Therapy:

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT): This type of therapy helps children learn to manage their emotions and reactions. According to the research by Linke et al., exposure-based CBT can be effective in treating DMDD by helping children gradually face and manage situations that trigger their outbursts.
