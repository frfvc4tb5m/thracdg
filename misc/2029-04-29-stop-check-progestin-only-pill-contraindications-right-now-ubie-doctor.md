What are the contraindications for progestin-only birth control pills?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Progestin-only birth control pills (also called mini-pills) have very few contraindications, making them a safer option for many people who can't take combination pills. However, they may not be suitable for individuals with certain health conditions, including current or past breast cancer, active liver disease, unexplained vaginal bleeding, or a history of certain types of cysts or tumors.

Because everyone's health profile is different, symptoms like irregular bleeding, breast changes, or abdominal discomfort shouldn't be ignored—especially when starting or considering hormonal contraception. Explanation

Progestin-only birth control pills are a type of contraceptive that is generally safe for many people to use. However, there are some situations where they may not be recommended. For example, individuals with a history of breast cancer or active liver disease should avoid using these pills.

Key Contraindications

Breast Cancer: If someone has or has had breast cancer, using progestin-only pills may not be safe.

Liver Disease: Active liver disease can make these pills unsuitable.
