# Sugar Ray Singer Mark McGrath Confirms He’s Had Hearing Loss, But Still Won’t Protect His Ears

Remember the scene in last year's A Star Is Born when the perspective shifts to that of Jackson Maine, played by Bradley Cooper, as he steps off stage following a dynamic show? We're slowly transported into the rock star's head, and a loud, constant, pestering ringing persists over all else, long after the guitars are done playing and the amps are unplugged.

That sensation might turn out to be more and more of a common phenomenon, as musicians who open themselves up to extremely loud and potentially damaging circumstances on a nightly basis deal with potential long-term consequences. The latest to find himself in this unfortunate situation is Mark McGrath, the singer best known for fronting the memorable 90s outfit Sugar Ray. In a recent post-show interview with the Daily Mail TV (via the Detroit Free Press) McGrath said that his hearing has in fact been failing him.

I can't hear anymore. I’ll be listening to people, and they'll tell me their names, and I can't hear anymore. It's scary because my job is hearing."

“Fortunately for the new generation, they have in-ears. I'm dumb enough not to use them though," he continued. "I still need the (sounds) and smells of a concert, and that includes the auditory part, but unfortunately, mine's going."

In further discussion on Daily Mail's show, however, it was said that the frontman has been working with his doctors to ensure that he keep the remaining hearing that he has left.

As the story continued to gain traction, McGrath did feel the need to calm the nerves of some fans who worried for the singer's health and performing future. When watching video of the comments, it's clear that he's speaking informally about a spur-of-the-moment hearing lapse, rather than making the big declaration that it initially may have seemed to be.

He took to Twitter to clarify, responding to an ET Canada tweet, saying "Though it’s true I’ve lost some hearing in one ear (a common occurrence in this profession), this headline is entirely misleading, and taken way out of context...Fortunately, (or unfortunately to some!) I look forward to performing for many years to come! Thanks for the concern"

He continued on when the headline was adjusted: "I really appreciate you doing that...because I’ve really been overwhelmed by the concern, kindness and support of those who think I’m actually deaf!Thank you again"

While McGrath's hearing concerns may not be quite as grave as it initially seemed, it's still strongly suggested that everyone exposed to loud noises—musicians, fans, and whoever else—be sure to take care and caution of your ears, because once hearing is lost, it's not coming back.
