EXPERT OPINION: Dr. Dulcinea Alex Pitagora, PhDPeople who are asexual do not disappear when unrecognized by the US Government; instead, they experience poorer physical and mental health and wellbeing, due to being mistreated, rendered invisible, and their needs being unmet. Also, asexuality is not analogous with sexual dysfunction and does not need to be treated as a disorder. Accurate and affirming resources on asexuality are crucial for the public and professionals alike, in order to support the wide range of sexual identities and orientations that exist, including asexuality. When accurate resources on asexuality aren’t available, internalized stigma and sexual shame increase among asexual individuals, as does the likelihood of mistreatment by peers and health care professionals. Additional resources, information, and community events can be found via organizations like Aces & Aros and Ace Recommended. Local events can be found via Meetup, in Facebook groups, and through local LGBT community centers. Often the best support comes from word of mouth, through making connections within your community.—Dr. Dulcinea Alex Pitagora, PhD, LCSW, CST is a NY-based psychotherapist and sex therapist.
