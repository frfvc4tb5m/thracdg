What Do Test Sensitivity And Specificity Mean?

Test sensitivity and test specificity refer to the spectrum that a medical test uses to determine whether an individual is considered ‘sick’ or ‘healthy’. This spectrum is designed to have a threshold that is low enough to exclude healthy people and high enough that it includes sick people.

Test sensitivity: The proportion of sick people who the test identifies correctly.

Test specificity: The proportion of healthy people that the test correctly identifies.

Tests with low sensitivity and/or specificity are more likely to give incorrect results such as false positives and false negatives. Tests with high sensitivity are less likely to give these incorrect results.

False positives can lead to additional costs, tests, and fear. False negatives, on the other hand, may lead you to not seek further treatment since you do not think you are sick. In addition, false negatives can cause an unintentional spread of the virus. That’s why getting a test with high sensitivity and specificity is important.

What are false positive and false negative results?

False positive: The person tests positive even though they are not sick. False positives can sometimes occur if a person is sick with a coronavirus strain other than SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19).

False negative: Even though the test comes back negative, the person is actually sick.
