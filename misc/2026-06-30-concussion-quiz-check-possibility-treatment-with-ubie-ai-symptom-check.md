What is Concussion?

Concussion is a brain injury caused by a blunt blow to the head or a violent shaking of the head and body. The effects are usually temporary.

Typical Symptoms of Concussion

Unable to remember what I have done

Fainting

Losing consciousness during a physical activity

Injury to the head

Dizziness

Recent changes in mental state

Headache

Difficulty concentrating

Diagnostic Questions for Concussion

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Have others mentioned increased forgetfulness?

Have you fainted recently?

Did you faint while walking or doing something?

Did you injure your head?

Do you feel dizzy?

Treatment of Concussion

There's no specific cure for concussion. Adequate rest and limiting screen time allows the brain to recover. Medication may be prescribed for headache, nausea, and other symptoms.

Scared by the Score? Why the Glasgow Coma Scale Predicts Recovery & Your Medical Next Steps

A.

There are several factors to consider: the Glasgow Coma Scale scores consciousness from 3 to 15 to classify injury severity (13 to 15 mild, 9 to 12 moderate, 8 or below severe), and trends over the first 24 to 72 hours help predict recovery, but outcomes also depend on age, imaging, treatment speed, and rehab access. Next steps range from urgent emergency care and ICU support for severe scores to follow up, brain rest, symptom tracking, and rehabilitation for milder injuries; see the complete guidance below to learn key details that could change what you do next.
