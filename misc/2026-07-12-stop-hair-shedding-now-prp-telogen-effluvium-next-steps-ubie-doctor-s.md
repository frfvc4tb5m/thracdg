Why Is Your Hair Shedding? Understanding Telogen Effluvium and PRP Treatment

Hair shedding is a normal part of the hair growth cycle—most people lose around 50–100 hairs per day. However, if you notice clumps of hair on your pillow, in the shower drain or an overall thinning of your hair, it may signal an underlying issue. One common cause is telogen effluvium, a type of temporary hair loss often triggered by stressors to the body. In this guide, we'll explain why hair sheds, what telogen effluvium involves, how PRP for telogen effluvium may help, and what medical next steps you can take.

1. Common Reasons for Increased Hair Shedding

Hair growth follows three phases:

Anagen (growth)

Catagen (transition)

Telogen (resting and shedding)

When more hairs than usual enter the telogen phase at once, you experience noticeable shedding. Common triggers include:

Physical or emotional stress

Illness or surgery

Hormonal shifts (postpartum, thyroid issues, menopause)

Typically, shedding begins 2–3 months after a trigger and may last up to six months. Recognizing the cause is key to effective treatment.

2. What Is Telogen Effluvium?

Telogen effluvium (TE) is a diffuse, non-scarring hair loss where a sudden shift in the hair cycle pushes hairs into the resting (telogen) phase. Features of TE:

Diffuse thinning over the entire scalp rather than in patches

Sudden onset of noticeable shedding

Often reversible once the trigger is removed

Because TE is not permanent, hair usually regrows within 6–12 months once the underlying cause is addressed.

3. PRP for Telogen Effluvium: How It Works

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy harnesses your body's own healing factors to stimulate hair growth:

Blood draw: A small amount of your blood is taken.

Centrifugation: The blood is spun to concentrate platelets.

Injection: The platelet-rich plasma is injected into areas of thinning scalp.

Platelets release growth factors that:

Encourage blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)

Activate stem cells in hair follicles

Extend the anagen (growth) phase

Evidence and Expectations

Multiple small studies show increased hair density and thickness after PRP for telogen effluvium.

Results vary; some patients see improvement in 3–6 months.

Maintenance sessions every 3–6 months may be needed.

While PRP is promising, it's not magic. It works best when combined with correction of underlying triggers (e.g., nutrition, hormone balance).

4. Medical Next Steps: Diagnosis and Treatment Plan

If you suspect telogen effluvium, these are the typical medical steps:

Detailed History and Exam

Onset and pattern of shedding

Recent illnesses, surgeries, stressors

Diet, medications, family history of hair loss

Basic Laboratory Tests

Complete blood count (CBC)

Ferritin (iron stores)

Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

Vitamin D levels

Hormone panels (if indicated)

Scalp Examination

Dermoscopy to rule out scarring alopecia

Pull test (gently tugging small sections of hair)

Address Underlying Causes

Correct nutritional deficiencies

Adjust or change medications if possible

6. When to Seek Immediate Help

Though telogen effluvium itself is not life threatening, serious conditions can mimic hair shedding:

Rapid, patchy hair loss (alopecia areata)

Signs of infection (redness, pain, pus) on the scalp

Unexplained weight loss, fatigue, fevers

Severe nutritional deficiencies
